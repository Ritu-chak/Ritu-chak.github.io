01 / About

Immune regulation
at the heart of disease.

I am an immunologist and translational researcher working at the intersection of innate immunity and cardiovascular pathology. My work focuses on Natural Killer T (NKT) cells — particularly the non-invariant subset — and their underexplored roles in atherosclerosis and vascular inflammation.

Currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at Johns Hopkins School of Medicine, I use advanced spectral flow cytometry and human clinical samples to bridge mechanistic discoveries with translational insights. I completed my Ph.D. at IIT Kharagpur, where I conducted extensive meta-analytic and experimental studies on NKT cell biology in atherogenesis.

02 / Research

Areas of Focus

⬡

NKT Cell Biology

Investigating the dual and divergent roles of invariant and non-invariant NKT cells in the development and progression of atherosclerotic plaques.

⬡

Vascular Immunology

Translational research on immune regulation in atherosclerosis and vascular inflammation using human samples and advanced spectral flow cytometry.

⬡

Lipid & Apolipoprotein Biology

Studying how ApoE modulates the number and activation state of non-invariant NKT cells, linking lipid metabolism to immune dysregulation.

⬡

Epigenetic Regulation

Characterizing how statins alter histone modifications (H3K4me3, H3K9ac) at the PCSK9 locus, revealing new epigenome players in hypercholesterolemia.

⬡

Meta-Analysis & Evidence Synthesis

Systematic meta-analyses of pre-clinical and clinical datasets using STATA to synthesize evidence on immune mediators in cardiovascular outcomes.

⬡

Cardiovascular Gene Therapy

Contributing to reviews on gene editing strategies in acquired and inherited cardiovascular disorders, including CRISPR-based approaches.
